# Game Modes and the Passage of Time

Most of the time during gameplay, {{players}} will be directly narrating their
{{characters}} intentions while the {{narrator}} actively negotiates the
ensuing actions and narrates their effects on the world, all without the
temporary structure of explicit rounds or turns. This mode of running the game,
however is just one {{game_mode}} called a {{scene}}.  In addition to
{{scenes}}, there are {{game_modes}} called {{tension}} and
{{ellipsis}}. Several {{scenes}}, {{ellipses}}, and periods of {{tension}}
combine together to form a {{chapter}} of the overall narrative of the game.


(gameplay:time:scenes)=
## Scenes: Unhuried Active Gameplay

{{Scenes}} are the most common form of gameplay in a typical Tu'urna
campaign. During a {{scene}}, there is no turn order or round structure, and
{{players}} and the {{narrator}} are free to interact as they see
fit. {{Scenes}} are intended to encompass short amounts of time, usually
ranging from a few minutes to a few hours. The {{narrator}} determines when a
{{scene}} begins and ends.

Some resources and {{abilities}} can only be used a certain number of times per
{{scene}}; these resources recharge as soon as a new {{scene}} begins.

A few examples of possible {{scenes}} follow:
* A short conversation in a bar with an informant.
* A ball at a noble estate that lasts several hours.
* A dinner party.
* A visit to a shop.
* An encounter with wild animals during travel.


(gameplay:time:tension)=
## Tension: When Timing is Critical

Sometimes during a {{scene}}, events occur such that the precise order and
timing of how things play out becomes critical for understanding how the
{{scene}} resolves. One common reason for {{tension}} is the advent of violence
(i.e., combat), but any event where timing is critical could call for
{{tension}}.

The {{narrator}} is in charge of determining when {{tension}} starts and ends
during any {{scene}}. {{Tension}} does not indicate that a {{scene}} is no
longer ongoing; rather, it changes the way the events of the {{scene}} are
tracked. {{Scenes}} do not end when {{tension}} starts or ends unless the
{{narrator}} determines that the {{scene}} has also ended.

A few possible examples of {{tension}} follow:
* Someone attacks someone else.
* Someone accuses a {{PC}} of a crime before a mob.
* A {{PC}} is attempting to encounter and speak with a spy during a dance
  at a masquerade ball.
* A {{PC}} realizes they are about to witness an assassination.

The rules of {{tension}} are detailed in the [next chapter](/gameplay/tension).


(gameplay:time:ellipsis)=
## Ellipsis: Time Spent Off-Camera

Frequently during a campaign, the {{characters}} spend time off-camera resting,
studying, planning, socializing, or any number of other activities.
"Off-camera" in this context simply means that the exact events are not
narrated in a high level of detail and large amounts of time can pass
relatively quickly. These times are called {{ellipses}}, and they are
determined by the {{narrator}}.

Resting and sleeping are the most common reasons for {{ellipsis}}, but a
few examples of other reasons for {{ellipsis}} follow:
* The {{PCs}} are undergoing a journey of many hours in a carriage.
* The {{PCs}} are at a specific location awaiting the arrival of another
  {{character}}.
* The {{PCs}} are forced to spend several weeks at an inn during a storm.

(gameplay:time:ellipsis:abilities)=
### Ellipsis Abilities
Only certain {{abilities}} can be used during {{ellipsis}}; for example, the
**Recover** {{ability}} of the [Fitness](skills:core:fitness) {{skill}} can be
used during {{ellipsis}} to remove or reduce the severity of a {{wound}}.
{{abilities}} that can be used during {{ellipsis}} are labeled as
{{ellipsis_abilities}} in their descriptions.

The [Fitness](skills:core:fitness) {{skill}} additionally requires that a
{{character}} who uses it spend 1 {{ellipsis_point}}.

{{Ellipsis_points}} are resources that are granted to {{PCs}} by the
{{narrator}} during {{ellipsis}}. The {{narrator}} decides how many
{{ellipsis}} points the {{PCs}} receive, usually based on the amount of time
being passed during the {{ellipsis}}. The following table provides a few
examples of {{ellipsis_point}} budgets for various game scenarios.

```{list-table} Examples of Ellipsis Point Rewards
:header-rows: 1

* - Number of {term}`Ellipsis Points<Ellipsis Point>`
  - Description
* - **1**
  - The {{PCs}} spend an hour having a meal at a tavern.
* - **2**
  - The {{PCs}} relax during a carriage ride that lasts a couple of hours.
* - **3**
  - The {{PCs}} pass an evening in a hideout awaiting a meeting.
* - **4**
  - The {{PCs}} pass an evening as guests of a noble family at their manor.
* - **5**
  - The {{PCs}} spend most of a day sightseeing or killing time in a town.
* - **6**
  - The {{PCs}} rest overnight at an inn.
* - **10**
  - The {{PCs}} spend a week traveling uneventfully with a caravan.
* - **15**
  - The {{PCs}} spend multiple weeks awaiting a festival in a city.
```

{{Ellipsis_points}} cannot be saved at the end of an {{ellipsis}}; any
unspent points are lost.

### What can Ellipsis Points Be Spent On?

{{Ellipsis_points}} are used for a variety of purposes, all of which are
enabled by specific {{abilities}}. For example, the Recover {{ability}} of the
[Fitness](skills:core:fitness) {{skill}} allows one to spend an
{{ellipsis_point}} to remove or reduce the {{severity}} of a {{wound}}. (Note
that this {{ability}} requires a single level of the
[Fitness](skills:core:fitness) {{skill}} to use, so it is automatically
accessible to all {{characters}}.) All {{abilities}} that requires the
{{character}} to spend an {{ellipsis_point}} can be used during {{ellipsis}}
and cannot be used except during {{ellipsis}}.

Typically, {{ellipsis_points}} can be spent the kinds of activities one would
typically perform during "downtime" in a traditional TTRPG. For example
recovering, studying, and working on various projects are all activities that
one can engage in by spending {{ellipsis_points}}. Like the activities that are
performed during {{scenes}}, many of the activities that can be performed
during {{ellipsis}} require additional materials or circumstances aside from a
period of {{ellipsis}}. For example, if a {{character}} wishes to spend part of
an {{ellipsis}} making or repairing clothing, they must have a needle and
thread. Each {{ability}} that can be used during {{ellipsis}} will describe its
requirements.
